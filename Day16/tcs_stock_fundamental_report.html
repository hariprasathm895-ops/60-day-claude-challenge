Company overview

Tata Consultancy Services (TCS) is India's largest IT services and consulting company and a flagship of the Tata Group. Founded in 1968 and headquartered in Mumbai, TCS operates across 55 countries with over 6,00,000 employees. It serves clients in banking, retail, manufacturing, telecom, and life sciences. CEO K. Krithivasan leads the company, which is dual-listed on NSE and BSE.

Where TCS stands out vs. peers

TCS leads peers on ROE (45.6% vs. Infosys ~32%) and commands the largest market cap, reflecting brand premium and client stability. Its P/E at 15.8x is now lower than all major peers (Infosys 24x, HCL 25.5x), an unusual inversion. Scale advantage (~6L+ employees, 55 countries) remains unmatched. The $220M legal charge is a near-term overhang not visible in peer books.

Ownership assessment

Promoter holding of 71.77% by Tata Sons reflects exceptionally high founder-group commitment — well above the 🚩 pledge flag threshold of >10% pledging, but here pledge is 0%. FII holding at 9.66% has likely declined due to global IT sector rerating and the stock's underperformance. DII accumulation at 13.41% suggests domestic institutional confidence. Shares outstanding reduced by 2.65% YoY, indicating active buybacks that support EPS. Overall ownership structure: High quality — no governance red flags.

3 fundamental strengths
Zero-debt, FCF machine
D/E of ~0 and FCF of ₹46,251 Cr TTM. Virtually no financial risk; cash flows fund 5.1% dividend yield and buybacks without strain.
Unmatched scale and client stickiness
Operations in 55 countries, 6L+ employees, and decades-long client relationships in BFSI, retail, and manufacturing — a wide economic moat with high switching costs.
ROE and ROCE far above average
ROE of 45.6% and ROCE of 54.9% place TCS in the top tier of Indian large-caps, reflecting a capital-light model with high operating leverage.
2 watch-points
Revenue growth slowdown
5-year sales CAGR of 10.2% is modest for a large-cap IT company. Global IT discretionary spending weakness and macro headwinds are suppressing deal ramp-ups. Monitor quarterly revenue growth for reacceleration signals.
$220M US legal exposure
DXC trade-secrets judgment upheld by US Supreme Court. TCS must provision $70M more in Q1 FY27 (exceptional charge). Though manageable given TCS's balance sheet, this creates short-term earnings noise and reputational risk in US market.
